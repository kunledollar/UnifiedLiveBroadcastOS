# UBOS v5.11.2 — Production-Safe Alerting, Incident Response, Escalation, and Operational Runbooks

## Status

Planned

---

# Vision

Transform UBOS alerts from passive notifications into a coordinated operational-response system that helps production teams detect incidents, assign ownership, follow approved recovery procedures, escalate unresolved failures, communicate status, and verify recovery.

UBOS must be able to answer:

```text
What requires attention now?

How serious is it?

Who owns the response?

What has already been attempted?

Which recovery procedure should be followed?

When should the incident escalate?

Is the production safe to continue?

Has the incident actually been resolved?
```

This phase converts monitoring signals into structured, auditable production operations.

# Severity Definitions

## Sev0 — Emergency

Immediate threat to safety, emergency communication, regulatory obligations, or organization-wide production control.

Examples:

* unauthorized emergency broadcast
* compromised production-control authority
* active security breach affecting live outputs
* emergency communication unavailable
* multiple production sites lost

Response expectation:

```text
Immediate

Executive and emergency escalation
```

---

## Sev1 — Critical

Major live-production outage or severe public impact.

Examples:

* Program output lost
* all public streams offline
* recording and stream failure simultaneously
* control split-brain risk
* primary and standby failure
* broadcast interruption exceeding policy threshold

Response expectation:

```text
Immediate acknowledgement

Continuous response until stabilized
```

---

## Sev2 — Major

Significant degradation with service still partially available.

Examples:

* one major destination offline
* Program audio degraded
* primary recording failed but backup is active
* automation unavailable
* replay system lost during sports production
* major contribution source unavailable

---

## Sev3 — Moderate

Operational issue with limited production impact.

Examples:

* secondary output failure
* one noncritical source disconnected
* delayed graphics data
* standby replication warning
* backup verification overdue

---

## Sev4 — Informational

Low-risk issue or condition requiring review.

Examples:

* capacity advisory
* noncritical plugin warning
* planned maintenance notice
* expired test credential
* minor configuration drift

---

# Incident State Meaning

## Detected

A condition has been identified but not formally accepted as an incident.

## Open

The incident has been created and requires ownership.

## Acknowledged

An authorized responder has accepted responsibility.

## Investigating

The team is determining the cause and scope.

## Mitigating

Recovery or containment actions are underway.

## Monitoring

A fix or workaround has been applied and is being observed.

## Stabilized

Immediate production risk is controlled, though full recovery may remain incomplete.

## Resolved

Required health checks have passed and impact has ended.

## Closed

Review and documentation requirements have been completed.

# Alert Correlation

Related alerts should be grouped into one incident.

Example:

```text
Encoder queue growing

Stream bitrate falling

Destination packet loss rising

Output health degraded
```

These may be symptoms of:

```text
Primary uplink congestion
```

UBOS should avoid opening four independent incidents where one root cause is likely.

* production ID
* component dependency

* shared network interface
* destination
* time window
* trace ID
* command correlation ID
* topology relationship
* matching failure signature
* prior incident patterns

# Deduplication

Repeated alerts for the same continuing condition should update an existing alert rather than create new incidents.

Example:

```text
Program Audio Silent
```

Repeated every second should become:

```text
Active for 24 seconds

Observed 24 times
```

not 24 separate alerts.

# Escalation Conditions

Escalation may occur when:

* no acknowledgement
* no owner
* severity increases
* Program remains unavailable
* recovery step fails
* incident exceeds duration target
* required role is unavailable
* audience impact expands
* backup systems fail
* security impact is suspected

---

# Escalation Suppression

Escalation may be paused only when:

* incident is acknowledged
* an approved maintenance window applies
* a higher-level incident already owns the condition
* a supervisor explicitly suppresses it
* the incident is in validated monitoring state

Suppressions must be time-limited and audited.

# Recovery Verification

An action succeeding technically does not prove the incident is resolved.

Example:

```text

```

Resolution also requires:

```text
Destination receiving frames

Bitrate stable

Audio and video present

No repeated reconnect

External playback confirmed
```

# Acceptance Criteria

UBOS v5.11.2 is complete when:

* Alerts from UBOS services and approved external systems are normalized into a common operational model.
* Repeated alerts are deduplicated without hiding duration or recurrence.
* Related alerts can be correlated into a single incident.
* Incidents have severity, ownership, status, affected resources, and response deadlines.
* Sev0 and Sev1 incidents trigger major-incident workflows.
* Incidents can be acknowledged, assigned, transferred, merged, split, stabilized, resolved, closed, and reopened.
* Escalation policies route incidents when acknowledgements, ownership, or recovery targets are missed.
* On-duty schedules and fallback responders are supported.
* Escalation suppression is visible, temporary, authorized, and audited.
* Versioned operational runbooks support manual, guided, conditional, parallel, automated, and rollback steps.
* Runbook actions pass through authorization, approval, command, and audit controls.
* Automated remediation is limited by declared risk and policy.
* Runbook state survives restart, control failover, and disaster recovery.
* Remediation commands are idempotent and reconciled before retry.
* Incident resource locks reduce conflicting recovery actions.
* Incidents cannot be resolved until required health checks and stability windows pass, unless an authorized override is recorded.
* Recurring failures can automatically reopen incidents.
* Internal and external status communication follows approved templates and update schedules.
* Incident timelines combine alerts, actions, decisions, notes, approvals, communications, and health evidence.
* Severe incidents require post-incident review.
* Reviews distinguish confirmed causes, probable causes, and unknown causes.
* Corrective actions have owners, deadlines, status, and verification criteria.
* Incident metrics measure detection, acknowledgement, mitigation, stabilization, and resolution performance.
* Alert storms and integration failures do not destabilize live production.
* Every important response action remains attributable and auditable.
