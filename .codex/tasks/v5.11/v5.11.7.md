# UBOS v5.11.7 — Production-Safe Compliance, Governance, Risk Management, and Policy Enforcement

## Status

Planned

---

# Vision

Introduce a unified governance, risk, and compliance layer that ensures UBOS productions, operators, automations, integrations, infrastructure, and security controls operate according to approved organizational, contractual, regulatory, and production policies.

UBOS must be able to answer:

```text
Which policies apply to this production?

Are required controls currently active?

What risks could affect this broadcast?

Are we compliant with contractual and internal obligations?

Which exceptions have been approved?

Which controls have failed?

Can this production safely proceed?

What evidence proves compliance?
```

This phase transforms governance from static documentation into active, measurable, and enforceable production behavior.

# Enforcement Mode Meaning

## Document Only

The policy exists for reference but is not evaluated automatically.

## Observe

UBOS evaluates compliance and records results but does not interrupt operation.

## Warn

Operators receive visible warnings when the policy is not satisfied.

## Require Acknowledgement

The operator must acknowledge the deviation before proceeding.

## Require Approval

An authorized approver must permit the action.

## Block

The action or production cannot proceed while the requirement remains unsatisfied.

## Automatic Remediation

UBOS may execute approved corrective actions automatically.

# Acceptance Criteria

UBOS v5.11.7 is complete when:

* Policies can be created, reviewed, approved, versioned, activated, suspended, superseded, and retired.
* Policies can apply by organization, customer, production, environment, site, region, destination, content type, role, and contract.
* Requirements map to one or more technical, procedural, administrative, physical, or contractual controls.
* Controls can be manual, assisted, semi-automated, automated, or continuously evaluated.
* Control tests support automated queries, synthetic tests, evidence reviews, attestations, and manual inspection.
* Evidence is collected with provenance, integrity, freshness, access control, and retention.
* Missing, expired, or invalid evidence reduces compliance status and confidence.
* Compliance can be evaluated by production, customer, policy, control, site, environment, and framework.
* Compliance results distinguish compliant, compliant with exceptions, partial, noncompliant, unknown, and not applicable states.
* Critical control failures remain visible even when aggregate scores appear high.
* External and internal frameworks can be mapped to reusable UBOS controls.
* Risks can be identified, assessed, owned, scored, treated, accepted, reviewed, and closed.
* Inherent and residual risk are tracked separately.
* Risk-appetite thresholds can require escalation, approval, or production blocking.
* Production risk profiles can be generated before going live.
* Governance gates can allow, warn, require approval, require remediation, or block production.
* Runtime policy enforcement continues after production start.
* Policy conflicts are resolved deterministically with visible rationale.
* Exceptions are scoped, time-limited, risk-assessed, approved, auditable, and automatically expired.
* Compensating controls can be required for approved deviations.
* Policy violations create findings, enforcement actions, incidents, and corrective actions where required.
* Corrective actions require ownership, deadlines, verification criteria, and closure evidence.
* Policies, controls, evidence, risks, exceptions, findings, attestations, vendors, and reviews are available in a unified governance dashboard.
* Customer-specific contracts can map to policies, controls, obligations, and reports.
* Governance obligations can track notification, reporting, review, and remediation deadlines.
* AI services can be governed by model, data scope, approved use, authority level, and human-approval requirements.
* Recovery sites enforce equivalent policy and data-residency restrictions.
* Governance state survives restart, HA failover, and disaster recovery.
* Policy evaluation, enforcement, exceptions, certifications, and risk decisions are fully authorized and audited.
